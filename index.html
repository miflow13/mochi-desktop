What’s new in v0.3

Growing Together.

v0.3 makes Mochi feel less like a collection of reactions and more like one persistent little companion.

Feed Mochi

Share a snack.

Feed Mochi from the context menu and watch his authored eating animation, sound, and post-snack reaction. Feeding contributes to Bond without turning care into a chore.

Emote Catalogue

He learns new tricks.

Bond levels unlock new expressions and emotes. The catalogue shows what Mochi knows, what’s still locked, rarity, and animated previews.

Focus with Mochi

Work beside each other.

Set focus blocks, breaks, rounds, gentle encouragement, and optional local rain. Mochi settles into a working loop while your session keeps its own clock and lifecycle.

Ambient companion

Still paying attention.

Typing, coding, terminal use, media, music, file browsing, idle time, and other broad contexts can shape what Mochi does — without reading your work.

Bond system

Progress without punishment.

Bond grows from meaningful shared activity: typing, Focus time, feeding, and simply using Mochi as part of your desktop routine. It never decays because you got busy, closed the app, or skipped a day.

AmbiSense · privacy first

Context, not content.

Mochi can react to the broad shape of desktop activity while deliberately avoiding the private details of what you’re doing.

⌨

Anonymous typing pulses

Activity timing and intensity only. No key symbols, characters, words, or typing history.

◫

Broad app categories

Contexts such as editor, terminal, browser, media, or pixel-art software — not document names or screen contents.

⌂

Local behavior

AmbiSense is a lightweight local rule-based system. It is not an LLM or cloud AI service.

Built for Linux

Early public alpha

Give Mochi a corner of your desktop.

Fedora + GNOME + Wayland is the primary tested setup. The installer creates Mochi’s private Python environment, installs the GNOME helper, and adds Mochi to your app grid.

Odd behavior is useful information.

Mixed monitor layouts, fractional scaling, workspaces, XWayland, and non-GNOME environments can still expose edge cases. If Mochi acts weird, a detailed issue report genuinely helps.
